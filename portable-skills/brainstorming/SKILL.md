# Brainstorming Skill

## Purpose
Generate a large volume of diverse ideas without premature judgment, explore unconventional combinations, challenge obvious assumptions, and maximize the solution space before narrowing down.

## When to Use
- Starting a new feature, product, or campaign with an open-ended problem
- Breaking out of groupthink or when standard approaches feel stale
- Exploring multiple directions before committing engineering or design effort
- Naming, positioning, workflow ideation, or alternative architecture exploration

## Behavioral Guidelines
1. Generate many ideas before evaluating or filtering any of them.
2. Explore unconventional, counterintuitive, or provocative approaches.
3. Combine unrelated concepts, patterns, or domains to spark novel solutions.
4. Challenge "obvious" assumptions and default constraints about the problem space.
5. Defer judgment during the ideation phase—avoid shutting down ideas prematurely.
6. Maximize the breadth and variety of possibilities explored before converging.

## Workflow
1. **Reframe the Prompt**: State the core goal without baking in a preconceived solution.
2. **List & Invert Assumptions**: Write down what everyone assumes must be true, then ask "What if the opposite were true?"
3. **Diverge Rapidly**: Produce a wide list of ideas across distinct categories (safe, bold, low-cost, radical).
4. **Cross-Pollinate**: Borrow mechanisms from unrelated industries, nature, or games and apply them to the problem.
5. **Cluster & Highlight**: Group raw ideas by theme and mark the most promising candidates for a separate evaluation phase.

## Output Expectations
- High volume of distinct ideas (typically 10–20+) organized by angle or theme
- Mix of practical variations and unconventional/wildcard concepts
- Explicit assumption challenges that open new directions
- Clear separation between divergent generation and later evaluation

## Limitations
- Does not filter for feasibility, cost, or correctness on its own
- Can overwhelm decision-makers if not followed by an analytical or optimization pass
- Unsuitable when a single deterministic procedure or urgent fix is required

## Example
**Prompt**: "How can we reduce user drop-off during our 5-step onboarding?"

**Response** *(Divergent Ideation — Unfiltered)*:
1. **Eliminate Onboarding**: Drop users straight into a pre-populated sandbox project.
2. **Reverse Flow**: Let users achieve one small win first, then ask for account details.
3. **Concierge Mode**: Replace forms with a 3-question conversational setup.
4. **Template Marketplace**: Let users pick a community template as step 1.
5. **Progressive Unlock**: Hide advanced settings until day 3; only ask for email + goal.
6. **Interactive Video/Walkthrough**: Turn step 1 into a 30-second playable demo.
*(Next step: evaluate top 2–3 ideas with Analytical or Experimental skills.)*
